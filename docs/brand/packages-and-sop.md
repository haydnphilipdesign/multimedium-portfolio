### A5. Terms [PROPOSED]

1. **Payment:** 50% to reserve your start date, 50% before launch (before the domain is switched over). The same split applies at every package level.
2. **The schedule starts** when the content questionnaire and your logo, photos, and access details are received.
3. **A revision round** is one consolidated list of feedback, sent together. Extra rounds are billed at the hourly rate.
4. **Pauses:** if the project waits on you for more than 30 days, it's paused and rescheduled into the next available slot. Nothing is lost.
5. **Scope changes** (new pages or features mid-project) are quoted in writing before work starts.
6. **Cancellation:** the deposit covers work already scheduled and started and is non-refundable once work begins.
7. **Ownership:** once paid in full, you own your content, your domain, and your site's design and code. (I keep the right to reuse my own general-purpose code and to show the project in my portfolio unless you ask me not to.)
8. **Leaving hosting:** you get a complete copy of your site files plus a 30-minute handoff call at no charge. Migration help beyond that is billed hourly.
9. **Accounts:** your domain and third-party tools are registered in *your* name. I get access as a collaborator.

## Part B — Project SOP

Each step lists what happens, who does it, and the template to use. Templates are short on purpose: edit them to sound like you.

### Step 1 — Inquiry arrives (form → email)
- Reply **within one business day**. Check `source` and `package` in the email to see where they came from.
- **Good fit:** real estate business, budget of $750+, timeline that works. Go to Step 2.
- **Not a fit:** reply kindly and point them somewhere useful.

> **Template: first reply**
> Hi [Name], thanks for reaching out about a website for [Business]. From what you wrote, it sounds like [one-line restatement of their need]. A [package] would likely fit, but I'd like to ask a few questions first so I recommend the right scope. Would a 20-minute call work this week? Here's a link to pick a time: [booking link]. If you'd rather keep it to email, just reply with [2–3 specific questions].
> — Haydn

### Step 2 — Fit call (20–30 minutes)
**Agenda:**
1. What the business does and who it serves (agents? buyers and sellers? investors?)
2. What's not working with the current site, or why now
3. What visitors should do on the site (start a file? request a consultation?)
4. Pages and features they expect, and the tools they already use (form tool, CRM, MLS)
5. Timeline, budget, and who decides

End the call with: "I'll send a proposal by [date, within 2 business days]."

### Step 3 — Proposal (within 2 business days)
Contents: the problem in their words · package + page list · copywriting responsibility · forms and integrations · timeline · revision rounds · price and payment split · what's not included · hosting & care · a link to the case study that matches their package.

### Step 4 — Agreement & deposit
Signed agreement (terms from A5) + 50% invoice. Book the start date only after the deposit is paid.

### Step 5 — Kickoff
Send the **content questionnaire** (below), request the logo, photos, brand colors, reviews they want to use (with links to the originals), and collaborator access to the domain and form tool.

> **Content questionnaire (core questions)**
> 1. In one or two sentences, what do you do and for whom?
> 2. List your services. For each: what's included, what it costs (if you publish pricing), and who it's for.
> 3. Why do clients choose you over others? Give specific examples.
> 4. What's the exact next step you want visitors to take? (Start a file / request a consultation / call)
> 5. What questions do new clients always ask you?
> 6. Areas you serve, licenses or designations, and anything you must display for compliance (e.g., brokerage name, license numbers, Equal Housing logo)
> 7. Three websites you like and why. One you dislike and why.
> 8. Reviews or testimonials you can share, with a link to the original
> 9. Team members to feature (name, role, photo, two-line bio)
> 10. Anything we must avoid saying

### Step 6 — Structure & copy
Sitemap + a full copy draft. Client review = copy round 1 (and round 2 if needed). **Copy is approved before design starts.** That avoids redesigning around changed words.

### Step 7 — Design
Design the homepage first, review it (design round 1), then the remaining pages, and review those (design round 2).

### Step 8 — Build
Build on a private preview link and connect the forms. Send a test submission to the client's inbox and have them confirm receipt.

### Step 9 — Pre-launch checklist
- [ ] Every page checked on phone, tablet, and desktop
- [ ] Every form submitted and delivered to the right inbox
- [ ] Page titles, descriptions, and social preview image set
- [ ] Contact details, license and compliance info correct
- [ ] No placeholder text or images; every quote and number is approved
- [ ] Accessibility scan clean; keyboard navigation works
- [ ] Analytics in place (if included)
- [ ] Final 50% invoice paid

### Step 10 — Launch & handoff
Switch the domain, re-test the forms on the live domain, and walk the client through the site (15–30 min). Send the handoff email: what's live, how to request edits, what hosting & care covers, and the fix-window end date.

### Step 11 — Follow-up (this is where proof comes from)
- **Day 14:** "Anything need adjusting?" (closes the fix window)
- **Day 21:** review request, linking to `https://www.multimedium.dev/review` (it asks for consent to publish)
- **Day 30–60:** case study request (see `docs/brand/case-studies.md`). Ask for any numbers they're willing to share, such as inquiries or files started.

> **Template: review request**
> Hi [Name], now that [site] has been live for a few weeks, would you share a few sentences about working together? It takes about two minutes: [review link]. It helps other [TCs / agents] decide whether I'm a good fit. Thank you!
